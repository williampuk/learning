Cooperative signing with MuSig2

Why a single signature can represent two people

Alice and Bob want both approvals for the normal payment without publishing a script naming both. Schnorr suggests an easy intuition: adding points and responses adds both sides of the verification equation. A secure protocol has to agree on the keys, message, nonces, and tweak before doing that.

P_A = d_A G;  P_B = d_B G
Naive aggregate: P_A + P_B
Rogue choice: P_B = X − P_A, making the result X

Here X is a point whose secret a malicious registrant knows. If the registrar simply adds unauthenticated keys, the attacker can choose the advertised point P_B from X and the honest party’s public point P_A. They do not need the discrete log of P_B to register that rogue key. The resulting aggregate is under the attacker’s sole control. Independent Schnorr signatures also have independent challenges, so their s values cannot simply be added to make a valid aggregate signature.

What MuSig2 adds

  1. Agree on keysUse the same ordered list and authenticated participant keys.
  2. Bind aggregateDerive key-specific coefficients from that list and compute the weighted group key.
  3. Exchange noncesEach signer generates a fresh secret pair and publishes its two nonce points.
  4. Sign and combineBind to the message, output key and combined nonce. Verify the final Schnorr signature. 
P_raw = Σ a_i P_i
R₁ = Σ R_i,1;  R₂ = Σ R_i,2
R_raw = R₁ + b·R₂
s_i = k_i + e·a_i·d_i          schematic, with normalized signs

The a_i are public scalars from the ordered compressed key list and each key, tagged as specified by BIP327. The specified second distinct key can use coefficient 1. Nonce coefficient b binds the serialized aggregate nonces, x coordinate of the final key, and message. Each participant’s effective secret nonce starts from k_i,1 + b·k_i,2 mod n. Signers apply the aggregate nonce’s even-Y sign and the key contributions’ accumulated parity signs. e is the BIP340 challenge for the final key and common message. The full protocol handles validation, infinity edge cases, and tweak bookkeeping. Treat the schematic as the algebra behind an audited implementation.

For one Taproot x-only tweak, let α be +1 or −1 to turn P_raw into even internal P. Let β be +1 or −1 to turn Q=P+tG into even Q. Then signing key contributions are β α a_i d_i, each reduced modulo n. Let γ be the sign turning the shared nonce into its even representative for ordinary non-infinity sessions. The signer uses k_i = γ(k_i,1 + b k_i,2) mod n. Public α, β, and γ come from point parity, while the nonce scalars and d_i stay private.

Σ s_i + e·β·t = k + e·q  (mod n)
qG = lift_x(x(Q));  kG = R (even Y)

The term e·β·t accounts for the public tweak, so final verification is ordinary BIP340. BIP327 specifies the exact two-round protocol, including partial signatures and safeguards. Each secret nonce is single-use even across aborts. A malicious coordinator can deny service. Signers should independently confirm the participant list, transaction, aggregate key and agreed tree. MuSig2 is an optional off-chain signing protocol. Consensus sees one public point and one Schnorr signature.

Spend together or recover alone

For normal payment, Alice and Bob agree on recipients and fee, derive the key-path digest for Q, and complete a tweaked MuSig2 session. They publish one signature. For recovery, Alice needs her separate leaf key, the exact leaf and its control block, and a spend with a mature relative sequence. The node first proves the leaf is authorized, then executes it. A script threshold differs from MuSig2: the recovery script performs separate signature checks on chain and discloses its key list.

Experiment I · Compare the vault journeys

The demonstration holds the stripped size fixed at 94 bytes and uses one input and one identical destination output in both cases. SegWit marker and flag add two bytes. The serialized key witness is 66 bytes, while the recovery witness is 172 bytes. The resulting weights are 444 and 550 WU, or 111 and 138 vB after rounding. The 106 WU difference belongs to this policy and shape.

Check your understanding

Level 1 · Can the node tell which wallet produced a MuSig2 signature from its witness?

Hint

Check what the witness encodes.

Worked solution

It sees a standard BIP340 signature for x(Q). Transaction context may provide clues, but the witness itself encodes no signer count or hidden policy.

Level 2 · Why does the coordinator add e·β·t?

Hint

Multiply the final response by G.

Worked solution

Each partial response accounts for one weighted internal secret contribution. The additional term supplies the normalized output tweak contribution so the result verifies under lift_x(x(Q)).

Level 3 · In the experiment, find the extra bytes that make the comparison 106 WU apart.

Hint

Count witness item prefixes as well as payload.

Worked solution

The recovery script adds 39 payload bytes plus one prefix. Control adds 65 bytes plus one prefix. Both sides use a one-byte item count and an identically sized signature item.
